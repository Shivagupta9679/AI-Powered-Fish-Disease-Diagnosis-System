Disease: Ich / White Spot Disease

Symptoms:
Small white spots on skin or fins, excess mucus, flashing/rubbing, lethargy, clamped fins, reduced appetite, and abnormal swimming.

Description:
A parasitic disease caused by Ichthyophthirius multifiliis, mainly affecting freshwater fish including salmonids. The parasite can infect the skin, fins, and gills.

Management:
Confirm infection by microscopic examination; use an appropriate approved antiparasitic treatment and improve water quality and system hygiene.

Prevention:
Quarantine new fish, avoid introducing infected fish or contaminated equipment, maintain good water quality, and reduce stress and overcrowding.


Disease: Furunculosis

Symptoms:
Skin redness, hemorrhages, ulcers or deep lesions, fin damage, lethargy, reduced appetite, abnormal swimming, and increased mortality.

Description:
A bacterial disease of salmonids caused by Aeromonas salmonicida. It can produce hemorrhages, tissue necrosis, skin ulcers, and systemic infection.

Management:
Confirm the bacterial cause through appropriate diagnostic testing; isolate affected fish and use antimicrobial treatment only under appropriate veterinary/aquaculture guidance.

Prevention:
Use healthy stock, quarantine new fish, maintain sanitation and good water quality, reduce stress and overcrowding, and use vaccination where appropriate.


Disease: Saprolegniasis

Symptoms:
White or gray cotton-like growth on the skin or damaged tissue, skin lesions, lethargy, reduced appetite, and abnormal swimming.

Description:
A water-mold infection caused by organisms such as Saprolegnia. It commonly develops as an opportunistic infection following skin damage, stress, or poor environmental conditions.

Management:
Correct poor water-quality conditions, minimize further skin damage, remove severely affected material when appropriate, and use an approved treatment under professional guidance.

Prevention:
Maintain good water quality and sanitation, reduce handling injuries and stress, avoid overcrowding, and quarantine affected fish.
